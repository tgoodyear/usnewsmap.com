# OCR gold set

The gold set is the human-transcribed text we score OCR engines against, so any engine, profile or route change can be measured by character error rate (CER) and word error rate (WER) on the same text ([#296](https://github.com/tgoodyear/usnewsmap.com/issues/296)). The [engine survey](../reports/2026-10-ocr-engine-survey/report.md) had no ground truth and could only use the damage metric; the gold set fills that gap. It has three parts:

- **Our own crops**: 60 crops from the survey's pages, plus 10 more boxes for Polish and Russian. None is transcribed yet, so they are scored as engine agreement only (`gold.py agree`). The 40 Czech, Serbian, Polish and Russian crops are out for transcription.
- **Public ground truth**: published datasets whose text people transcribed and checked, converted by `scripts/ocr-survey/gold.py` into the same crop layout. These give the accuracy numbers.
- **The volunteer bundle**: a fixed, checksummed subset of the public sets that BOINC hosts score their engine on.

To score an engine yourself, follow the [scoring guide](scoring-guide.md). The plan in [13 §13.9](../design/13-ocr-engines.md#139-how-the-survey-and-production-share-engine-images) names `score --truth <crops dir>`; the gold set is what goes in that directory. Trust calibration on the bundle is in the [gold check](../boinc/server/gold-check.md#host-trust-for-usnm_ocr) and, for its history, the notes on [the 80bf worker](../notes/2026-10-16-gold-bundle-80bf-measurements.md), [English calibration](../notes/2026-10-17-gold-english-trust-calibration.md) and [German Fraktur](../notes/2026-10-18-german-fraktur-diagnostic.md).

## Selection

- **Source.** The survey's 80 scored pages (`data/scores.jsonl`; the sample's images are not in the repository). One crop per page.
- **Target.** 60 crops, shared equally between the survey's groups; a group with fewer pages gives all of them and the others share the rest. The selection is 10 each for German, worst-English, Serbian, Czech and control, and 5 each for Polish and Russian (all their pages).
- **Strata.** Group (language and print type: the survey groups were the worst-read cases per language, and the control is well-read English dailies), then era. Inside a group the picks cycle through decades in a seeded order, so a group spans as many decades as it has pages. The era runs from the 1850s to the 1960s.
- **Seeded.** `gold.py select` with seed 296 reproduces `fixtures/ocr-gold/selection.jsonl`.
- **Not stratified by typeface.** The sample records no typeface (Fraktur or Antiqua, for German). Record it in `notes` when transcribing; if a stratum turns out to hold one typeface only, add pages.
- **Crop box.** One text region per page, chosen as the survey's `crops.py` does (a line of LoC's ALTO with 4 or more words in a block at least 600 pixels wide, and the region below it, up to 1,600 pixels wide and 6% of the page high), seeded per page. The box is fixed in the manifest.

The survey's `scores.jsonl` is in the `report-data` set, so fetch it first: `scripts/ops/data-assets.sh --fetch --set report-data` ([reports/README.md](../reports/README.md), "Data").

```sh
scripts/ocr-survey/gold.py select --scores docs/reports/2026-10-ocr-engine-survey/data/scores.jsonl \
  --out fixtures/ocr-gold/selection.jsonl --target 60 --seed 296
# where the survey's sample and work directories are:
scripts/ocr-survey/gold.py cut --selection fixtures/ocr-gold/selection.jsonl --sample SAMPLE --work WORK --out OUT
```

`select` with `--manifest SAMPLE/manifest-final.jsonl` gives the same choice and carries the image and ALTO URLs and checksums.

The 40 Czech, Serbian, Polish and Russian crops (ten each) were cut on 22 October 2026 with `gold.py cut`'s box rule at seed 296. Polish and Russian have five pages each, so each of those pages gives a second, non-overlapping box. Their manifest and the empty transcription template are in `fixtures/ocr-gold/cze-srp-pol-rus/` (`manifest.jsonl`, `crops.jsonl`; the crop images are not committed). Surya reads the same boxes as BOINC crop units, so its CER can be scored once the transcriptions come back: see [Surya on the gold crops](../boinc/apps/surya.md#surya-on-the-gold-crops-968).

## Crop format

- `manifest.jsonl`, one row per crop: `crop_id` (`<group>-<nn>`), `page_id`, `group`, `lang`, `era`, `stratum`, `lccn`, `date`, `edition`, `seq`, `title`, `box` (x0, y0, x1, y1 in the page image's pixels), `image`, `crop_sha256`, `encoder`, `page_url` (the loc.gov page), `page_image_url` (the IIIF full-size JPEG the survey used), `page_image_sha256`, `alto_url`.
- `images/<crop_id>.jpg`: the box cut from the page, grayscale, JPEG quality 85.
- `crops.jsonl`, the transcription template, one row per crop: `crop_id`, `group`, `lang`, `date`, `title`, `image`, `hints`, `transcription` (null until a person fills it in), `transcriber`, `reviewer`, `hint_copy_reviewed` (false), `notes`.
- `hints.texts` holds what LoC's ALTO and each engine read in the box, in visual line order. `hints.is_truth` is false and `hints.note` says so. A hint is there to find the passage on the page, not to be corrected. The CER of a hint against the transcription is a result, so a transcription that copies a hint spoils it.

## Where the images live

The page checksum pins the pixels. `page_image_sha256` is the checksum of the page JPEG the survey fetched, and `gold.py fetch` downloads each page (paced as the survey's `locfetch.py` does), checks it, and cuts the box. The crop JPEGs are committed under `fixtures/ocr-gold/` only if the total is under about 10 MB; `cut` prints the total. Otherwise the repository holds the manifest, and `fetch` rebuilds the images. JPEG bytes can differ between Pillow versions, so a differing `crop_sha256` with a matching page checksum is a warning, not an error. 60 pages are about 60 full-resolution downloads from loc.gov, so run `fetch` on a machine with the bandwidth and disk for them.

## Transcription rules

1. **Verbatim.** Type what is printed, including errors, old spellings, long s (ſ), ligatures and printed punctuation. Don't correct, modernize or normalize. Serbian stays in the script printed (Cyrillic or Latin).
2. **Lines.** One line of the transcription per printed line in the crop, in reading order for the crop's column. If the crop cuts through a line at its edge, transcribe only the characters wholly inside the crop and note it in `notes`.
3. **Hyphenation.** Keep the hyphen at the end of a line as printed and don't join the word. The scorer joins it (see [CER and WER](#cer-and-wer)).
4. **Uncertain characters.** `[?]` stands for one character that can't be read. `[?x]` stands for characters read as `x` with doubt. More than 2% of a crop's characters marked this way fails validation; drop the crop and add another page instead.
5. **Out of scope.** Leave out ornaments, rules and the text of images. Don't skip printed text inside the crop. The scorer can't align around a gap, so validation rejects a `[...]` marker; drop such a crop and add another page instead.
6. **Who.** The maintainer transcribes, or a person they name. Set `transcriber` to that person. An engine's text, or a model's reading of the image, is never the transcription.
7. **Second pass.** A different person reads every transcription against the image, fixes it, and sets `reviewer`. The validator rejects a reviewer equal to the transcriber. If only one person is available, run validate with `--allow-unreviewed`, and say so wherever scores are quoted.
8. **Hint copies.** A transcription equal to a hint after normalization fails validation. If the hint is right, check the image and set `hint_copy_reviewed` to true.

```sh
scripts/ocr-survey/gold.py validate --crops OUT/crops.jsonl --manifest OUT/manifest.jsonl --images OUT/images
```

- No script, log or note beside them or in the repository records how the references were made. `ocr/ja-ocr/tools/crops.py` says "transcribe each crop into refs/<page_id>.txt" and nothing more; the commit that added it has no message beyond its title.
- None is identical to any engine's text for the crop (best similarity per file 0.36 to 0.93 against NDLOCR-Lite, the prototype NDLOCR run, or Azure), so they are not copies of one engine's output.

So they are not verified as hand-made, and most likely machine-made. They are not ground truth: until a person transcribes them from the images, they are a hint, like an engine's text. The crop images are not in the repository, so they are not in the manifest yet.

## CER and WER

`usnm-ocr-survey score` (the `crates/usnm-ocr-survey` crate, [#457](https://github.com/tgoodyear/usnewsmap.com/issues/457)) compares each hypothesis text with the transcription. It replaces `gold.py score`, which stays in the repository as the reference: both take the same flags and write the same files, byte for byte. Fetching, converting and validating stay in `gold.py`. This is the CER the plan describes in 13 §13.9, minus the part that finds the crop's span in a whole page's text. For an engine that writes word boxes, `alto-crop` does that part: it clips the engine's ALTO to the crop's box (see [Word position against ALTO](#word-position-against-alto)). The arithmetic is edit distance over the normalized transcription's length, summed over crops (a crop's weight is its length), reported for all crops and per group. WER is the same over whitespace-separated words.

Characters are Unicode scalar values, as Python's `str` counts them, and whitespace is what Python's `str.isspace` accepts (Rust's `White_Space` plus U+001C to U+001F). The edit distance is Myers' bit-parallel algorithm in Hyyrö's multi-word form, about n·m/64 word operations instead of gold.py's n·m Python steps. On the 600 BLN600 excerpts against Gale's OCR (1,696,781 reference characters), `gold.py score` took 880 s and `usnm-ocr-survey score` 0.66 s, with identical output files.

**Golden test.** `scripts/ocr-survey/golden_score.py` writes seeded synthetic crops and hypotheses into `crates/usnm-ocr-survey/tests/golden/` with gold.py's output for three runs (`--hyp`, `--hyp` plus `--hints`, `--hints --allow-unreviewed`). The inputs cover the edge cases: every Python whitespace character, line-end hyphens with CR and tabs, nested guess markers, combining characters, characters outside the Basic Multilingual Plane, a rounding tie, quotes and commas in CSV fields, an empty group, and each validation failure. The Rust test `tests/golden.rs` must reproduce the files byte for byte (the rust CI job), and the infra job runs `golden_score.py --check` whenever gold.py changes, so a change to either scorer that the other doesn't share fails CI. After a deliberate change to gold.py, regenerate with `scripts/ocr-survey/golden_score.py --out crates/usnm-ocr-survey/tests/golden`.

Normalization, on both sides:

1. Unicode NFC. Case, punctuation, diacritics, long s and ligatures are not folded.
2. A hyphen at the end of a line, followed by the line break, is removed and the line joined. The survey's text does the same.
3. Line breaks and runs of whitespace become one space.

In the transcription only, `[?x]` counts as `x` and `[?]` is dropped.

Hypotheses are the hint texts in `crops.jsonl` (`--hints`), or per-engine files of `{"crop_id", "text"}` lines (`--hyp tesseract-5.5=FILE.jsonl`). Crops that fail validation are left out and counted.

```sh
cargo run --release -p usnm-ocr-survey -- score --crops OUT/crops.jsonl --hints --out OUT/scores
```

## Word position against ALTO

`usnm-ocr-survey alto-position` and `alto-crop` ([#1438](https://github.com/tgoodyear/usnewsmap.com/issues/1438), `crates/usnm-ocr-survey/src/position.rs`) score an engine by where each word sits on the page, so a misread word and a word read in the wrong place count apart. A text-order score mixes the two: an engine that reads every word right but takes two columns in the other order scores almost as badly as one that misreads them. American Stories' 130% CER on ALTO crop boxes in the October pilot came from article-level ordering.

**LoC's ALTO is not ground truth.** Run against it, these commands measure agreement with LoC's OCR. Where two engines differ, they say which one agrees with LoC, not which one is right; LoC's own errors count against an engine that read the word correctly. Accuracy numbers still come from human transcriptions (`score`, or `alto-crop --text-is-reference`). Agreement with LoC is the measure that scales to the whole corpus.

### Two ALTO files of one page

```sh
usnm-ocr-survey alto-position --reference LOC.xml --hypothesis ENGINE.xml [--list-unmatched]
```

Both files must be readings of the same page image with a box for every word; an ALTO with line boxes only is refused. Any ALTO `usnm-alto` reads will do, NDNP's early CCS ALTO included. Boxes are compared as proportions of the page, so LoC's 1/1200 inch and an engine's pixels need no dpi. Words come from the text blocks, normalized as `score` normalizes an engine's text (above), and a line's hyphen (ALTO's `HYP`) stays on the line's last word, so `Clare` with a `HYP` and `Clare-` are the same word.

**Matching.** Two words are candidates when their boxes' intersection over union (IoU) is at least `MIN_IOU`, 0.5. Candidates are taken greedily, the highest IoU first, with ties broken by the reference word's reading-order index and then the hypothesis word's. A pair is kept when neither word is matched yet, so matching is one-to-one, and the result depends only on the two files. Above 0.5 a box can match at most one of a set of boxes that don't overlap each other, so when neither page's word boxes overlap and no pair sits at exactly 0.5, the candidates are already one-to-one and the greedy order changes nothing. A box shifted sideways by more than a third of its width falls below 0.5, and so does a box covering two words of about the same width (two words read as one).

The output is one line of JSON:

| Field                                         | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `reference_words`, `hypothesis_words`         | Words on each side                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `matched`, `correct`, `substitutions`         | Pairs matched by position, and those with the same and with different text                                                                                                                       |
| `deletions`, `insertions`                     | Reference words with no match, and hypothesis words with no match                                                                                                                                |
| `wer`                                         | `(substitutions + deletions + insertions) / reference_words`                                                                                                                                     |
| `matched_chars`                               | Character edits within the matched pairs over the matched reference words' characters (`cer`): how well the engine read the words both found                                                     |
| `order.kendall_tau`                           | Kendall's tau between the two reading orders of the matched words: 1 in the same order, -1 backwards. Global: two columns read in the other order lower it by the share of word pairs that cross |
| `order.adjacent_in_order`                     | The share of consecutive matched words, in the reference's order, that the engine also reads in that order. Local: two columns read in the other order break one seam                            |
| `text_order`                                  | Each page's whole text in its own reading order against the other's, as a text-order score sees it, for comparison                                                                               |
| `unmatched_reference`, `unmatched_hypothesis` | With `--list-unmatched`: each unmatched word's index, text and box, in the reference's frame (`frame`)                                                                                           |

On the synthetic test page with two columns read in the other order, every word matches and is correct, `kendall_tau` is -0.14 and `adjacent_in_order` 0.86, and `text_order` has a CER of 0.9.

### A crop against its text

```sh
usnm-ocr-survey alto-crop --alto PAGE.xml --box X0,Y0,X1,Y1 --text CROP.txt \
  [--image-width W --image-height H] [--text-is-reference]
```

Engines that read crops (`usnm_qwen`, and `usnm_surya` while it ran) return text with no word positions, so their unit is the crop. `alto-crop` keeps the word-level ALTO's words whose box centre is in the crop's box, `x0 <= cx < x1` and `y0 <= cy < y1` (the rule of `crops.py` and `usnm_ocr_quality::crops`), and reads them in the ALTO's reading order, line by line as the crop shows them. A hyphen at the end of a kept line joins the next kept line's first word, even when the box cut off the word that followed it on the page: the crop's own text, normalized, joins the same pair. `--box` is in the page image's pixels when `--image-width` and `--image-height` give the image's size (the manifest's `box`), and in the ALTO's own units otherwise.

`text_order` is the CER and WER of the clipped words against the crop's text. By default the clipped ALTO is the reference and both are normalized as engine text. With `--text-is-reference` the text is a transcription, normalized as `score` normalizes one (`[?x]` read as `x`, `[?]` dropped), and the rates are over it. `bag` compares the two as bags of words (lower case runs of letters and digits, as [the text agreement](text-agreement.md) counts them): `alto_words`, `text_words`, `shared_words`, `cover_alto` and `cover_text` (the shared words over each side's) and `dice`. The bag ignores order, case and punctuation, so a high `text_order` CER beside a high `bag` overlap suggests the words are there in another order. It doesn't measure the ordering's share: a case or punctuation difference alone (ALTO `CAT` against text `cat`) gives the same gap.

## Public ground truth

Our own crops give agreement, not accuracy: two engines can agree and both be wrong. Accuracy needs a human transcription, so this section covers public datasets that have one. The maintainer will not hand-transcribe pages, though a named person may (see [Selection](#selection) for the 40 out for transcription).

The sources below were checked on 13 October 2026 from public documentation, dataset cards and repository metadata only; nobody was contacted. Anything not confirmed is marked UNVERIFIED. Licences are quoted from the source named in the table.

### Source policy

Other people use this repository, so every source used for scoring must be one anyone can check and that we can publish. A source is used for scoring only if all three hold:

1. **Public download.** Anyone can download it without a login, request form or agreement.
2. **High confidence.** The transcriptions were made or verified by people, and the source documents how.
3. **Use only under verified source terms.** No dataset content is committed, and fixtures are synthetic. `public-fetch` keeps source licence and attribution materials beside downloaded data (`SOURCE.json` plus the dataset's own licence files), and `public-convert` copies them into `LICENSES/`. NonCommercial datasets require the operator to verify that the specific use qualifies before serving them. For BLN600, the Figshare v2 record identifies CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Its linked deed and legal code condition sharing on NonCommercial use and attribution, and do not permit sharing adaptations. The workflow therefore mirrors only the unchanged original archive, preserves its `LICENSE` and `readme.md` with `SOURCE.json` and `ATTRIBUTION.txt`, and does not share derived BLN600 crops or re-encoded images. Volunteers cut crops locally ([Volunteer bundle](#volunteer-bundle)).

### What American Stories evaluated on

The [GitHub README](https://github.com/dell-research-harvard/AmericanStories) says: "Its vast size and silver quality make it ideal for innovation and research in this domain." It mentions no test data. The paper ([arXiv:2308.12477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12477), read as the [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.12477) through a summarising fetch tool, so check the wording against the paper before quoting it elsewhere) and the Hugging Face repositories say this:

- **Dataset card** ([dell-research-harvard/AmericanStories](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dell-research-harvard/AmericanStories)): licence CC-BY 4.0, data split by year, annotation section "Not Applicable", no test set, no CER.
- **Layout detector.** Trained on "2,202 labeled newspaper images, consisting of 48,874 layout objects". The labels were "created by undergraduate research assistants". "Scans from Chronicling America comprise 13% of the training sample", so most of the layout training data is not Chronicling America. The paper describes no held-out layout test split; it reports mAP 91.31 for articles and 88.64 for headlines.
- **Line OCR.** Compared on "64 textlines drawn randomly from random scans in the Chronicling America collection" (the Carlson et al. sample, CER 0.015 for the EffOCR-Word model the dataset used) and on a hand-transcribed "randomly selected sample of 50 lines for each decade between 1850-1920" (CER 0.043 over the whole sample; 8.9% in the 1850s to 1.8% in the 1910s). These line samples are not released in the repositories we found.
- **End to end.** "Two student annotators labeled layout regions and hand-entered the texts for 10 full page scans, resolving all discrepancies by hand. The set consists of 597 content regions and 196,655 characters." CER 0.051 (0.044 after spell-checking), which includes layout errors.
- **Where the human-labelled set is hosted.** [dell-research-harvard/AmericanStoriesTraining](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dell-research-harvard/AmericanStoriesTraining), folder `gold_data/`: `gold_hand_transcription.json` (COCO format, 471,216 bytes) and 10 page images (1.7 to 8.1 MB each, 44.4 MB in `gold_imgs.zip`). Repository metadata: licence `apache-2.0`; the card text is empty. Checked here, the JSON has 10 images, 597 annotations, 196,655 characters of text, matching the paper's numbers, so this is that set. Annotation classes include article (307 regions with text), headline (199) and others.
- **Not confirmed.** The paper does not say where the 10 pages come from or what years they cover. Given that Chronicling America is 13% of the layout training sample, they are not necessarily Chronicling America pages: UNVERIFIED. The README is "silver" about the full dataset; this 10-page set is the only hand-made evaluation text in the repositories.

The 10 pages are small but real ground truth, and they are public.

### Recommendation

1. **BLN600, for English.** The recommended English source: 600 human-transcribed excerpts of 19th-century London newspapers, each image already cut to the excerpt. ND licence, so nothing derived is shared.

3. **Reichsanzeiger-GT, for German Fraktur.** Line-level human transcription, page images fetched on demand, and a licence that allows redistribution under either statement. Its pages are a gazette, so layouts are denser and plainer than a daily paper's.

5. **NewsEye BnF, NLF Finnish and NLF Swedish, for French, Finnish and Swedish.**

### Fetch and convert

The [scoring guide](scoring-guide.md) has the commands for every dataset, the hypothesis format and how to read the results, including each dataset's transcription convention. This section records how the readers work and what the real runs gave.

`public-fetch` downloads a dataset to `--src`. It will not run without `--confirm-download`, because some downloads are large: the Reichsanzeiger images are 101 page JPEGs from Mannheim's server (paced one second apart, `--pages N` of them, 20 by default, in a fixed seeded order), the Austrian zips 183 MB, and the NDL zip 5.2 GB. Run it on any machine with network access and room for the files. `public-convert` reads what was downloaded and writes the layout `score` reads: `images/`, `manifest.jsonl`, `crops.jsonl`, with the dataset's transcription in `transcription`.

`--dataset` is `bln600`, `overproof-ca`, `reichsanzeiger`, `austrian`, `newseye-bnf`, `newseye-nlf-fi`, `newseye-nlf-sv` or `nzz` for scoring. The NewsEye sets take `--subset validation|training|all` (default `validation`, 16 to 122 MB; the Finnish training zip is 5.4 GB); `austrian` defaults to `all`. `bln600` checks the zip's sha256 before opening it; the zip is AES-encrypted with the password `BLN600` printed on the dataset page, which Python's zipfile can't read, so it needs `bsdtar` or 7-Zip. `overproof-ca` fetches loc.gov's issue JSON, then each page's ALTO and full image, paced as `locfetch` does, and `public-convert` writes the line match rate to `match.json`. Its crop geometry comes from matched ALTO strings. String text is retained even when its coordinates are invalid. Implausible dimensions are repaired from page medians when the TextLine bounds do not confirm them. Plausible TextLine bounds constrain String origins and extents and provide a fallback. Conversion fails if a matched nonempty line has no usable geometry. Matched lines are kept in one crop only while consecutive in ALTO and spatially adjacent; distant lines remain in separate crops rather than producing a page-spanning union. `overproof-smh`, `enp-berlin`, `americanstories` and `ndl-pdm` fail the [source policy](#source-policy); `public-convert` refuses them unless `--not-for-scoring` is given, and their rows then carry `for_scoring: false`. Don't quote their numbers as accuracy. A crop is one transcribed region (a block of lines for the first three, one line for NDL), chosen by seed: regions inside a length window (200 to 2,500 normalized characters by default; 200 to 8,000 for BLN600, 20 to 300 for NZZ lines and 12 to 300 for NDL lines; overridable with `--min-chars` and `--max-chars`), at most `--per-page` (3) per page, taking one from each page before a second from any. Each crop row carries `source_dataset`, `source_url` and `source_license`, and `transcriber` reads "`<dataset>`: published ground truth" with `reviewer` "not applicable: published ground truth", which is what lets `validate` accept it. Nobody here reviewed those transcriptions; the dataset's authors did, by the process they describe.

Unit tests run on tiny synthetic inputs for every reader, the pick, and the end-to-end conversion (the last one needs Pillow, so it is skipped where Pillow is missing). A real run of `public-convert` on the American Stories JSON and one of its page images (0015.jpg, 1.7 MB) cut readable crops that line up with their text. The Austrian training and validation archives converted for real on 18 October 2026 ([German diagnostic](../notes/2026-10-18-german-fraktur-diagnostic.md)); the publisher's two partitions overlap by newspaper issue. On the same day Mannheim's image server returned HTTP 503, so Reichsanzeiger conversion has not yet run on real images. The NDL reader follows the README's example and expects `json/<dir>/<name>.json` beside `img/<dir>/<name>.jpg`, UNVERIFIED until it runs on the real files.

## Volunteer bundle

BOINC volunteers score their engine on a fixed subset of the public sets, the gold bundle ([#457](https://github.com/tgoodyear/usnewsmap.com/issues/457)). It is versioned and checksummed, so every reported score names the exact crops it was measured on.

**Contents.** `usnm-ocr-gold-v<N>/` holds `manifest.json`, `crops/` and `licenses/<source>/`, and ships as a tar with a `.sha256` beside it. The manifest carries `bundle_version`, the seed, `files` (path, size and sha256 of every other file in the bundle), `sources` (licence, URL, attribution, how it ships, and its licence files), `remotes` (the original files the client downloads: URL, sha256, and the published password of BLN600's zip) and `crops` (id, source, language, group, where the image comes from and where the transcription comes from). The client reports `bundle_version` and the manifest's sha256 with every score.

**What ships is decided by the licence** (`bundle::policy` in the crate; a source not listed there can't go into a bundle):

| Source                                           | Licence                                  | Ships                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `bln600`                                         | CC BY-NC-ND 4.0                          | References only: the zip's URL and sha256, the image and transcription members' names and checksums. The client receives the unchanged zip from the project mirror and reads both unchanged. |
| `overproof-ca`                                   | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (text); LoC images       | The transcription and the box. The client downloads the LoC page image (URL and sha256 in the manifest) and cuts the box.                                                                    |
| `newseye-*`, `nzz`, `austrian`, `reichsanzeiger` | CC BY 4.0, CC0, CC BY-NC (NZZ's archive) | The crops and transcriptions.                                                                                                                                                                |

Every source's licence files go in `licenses/<source>/`, unchanged, with `SOURCE.json` and an `ATTRIBUTION.txt` that names the source, licence, licence deed, attribution and the changes made (for shipped crops: cut, converted to greyscale, re-encoded as JPEG). The builder refuses a source with no attribution; `gold.py` carries one for every scoring source. ShareAlike text (Overproof) ships with its licence and readme files. No BLN600-derived crop, image or text is included in the bundle. Its unchanged source `LICENSE` and `readme.md`, plus `SOURCE.json` and `ATTRIBUTION.txt`, are preserved under `licenses/bln600/`; the unchanged source zip is delivered separately.

**Building.** The bundle is built from `gold.py public-convert` outputs, reproducibly: the same inputs, version and seed give the same tar, byte for byte (no timestamps in the manifest; tar entries sorted, mtime 0, fixed modes). The pick spreads over pages: it puts the pages, and the crops in each page, in a seeded order, then takes them round robin, up to `--per-source`. Overproof needs `page_image_url` in its manifest rows, which `public-convert` writes from `loc/pages.json`.

```sh
cargo run --release -p usnm-ocr-survey -- bundle-build --version 1 --per-source 30 --out OUT/bundle \
  --source bln600=OUT/bln600 --original bln600=SRC/bln600/BLN600.zip \
  --source overproof-ca=OUT/overproof-ca --source newseye-bnf=OUT/newseye-bnf \
  --source newseye-nlf-fi=OUT/newseye-nlf-fi --source newseye-nlf-sv=OUT/newseye-nlf-sv --source nzz=OUT/nzz \
  --lock fixtures/ocr-gold/bundle-v1.lock.json
cargo run --release -p usnm-ocr-survey -- bundle-verify --bundle OUT/bundle/usnm-ocr-gold-v1.tar
```

The BOINC server can run the same build and check it against the lock, on the on-prem host with `usnm-boinc-step --step gold-bundle` ([on-prem server](../boinc/server/onprem.md)); the Azure server ran it inside its VNet: [Build the gold bundle](../boinc/server/gold-check.md#build-the-gold-bundle). `--original` is the downloaded archive of a reference-only source; the builder checks it against `SOURCE.json` and reads the members to record their checksums, and copies nothing from it. No dataset content is committed. The repository holds only the locks, `fixtures/ocr-gold/bundle-v1.lock.json` and `bundle-v2.lock.json`: version, seed, crop ids, file checksums, remotes, the manifest's and the tar's sha256.

**Version 1** (built 2026-10-13, seed 457, 30 per source): 142 crops, 30 BLN600, 30 Overproof CA (from 18 LoC pages), 24 NewsEye BnF, 18 NewsEye NLF Finnish, 10 NewsEye NLF Swedish and 30 NZZ lines. The tar is 9.6 MB (9.1 MB of shipped crops, a 140 KB manifest, 128 KB of licence files). A client also downloads 19 remotes: the 68 MB BLN600 zip and 18 page images. All 18 LoC page images matched the checksums recorded at conversion when fetched again on the same day.

**Version 2** (lock committed 26 October 2026, seed 457): the same six sources and 142 crops, rebuilt after #660 corrected Overproof's ALTO geometry, which changed 17 of the selected Overproof crop ids. A fresh v1 conversion no longer reproduces the v1 lock, and that check is kept; an existing verified v1 bundle is still accepted. Building or installing v2 submits no work and changes no trust. The build step and its hashes are in [Build the gold bundle](../boinc/server/gold-check.md#build-the-gold-bundle).

**Client.** `usnm-boinc gold-stage` (network) and `usnm-boinc gold-run` (offline), in [the gold check](../boinc/server/gold-check.md#gold-check). For `usnm_gold` (#462), the server-side prepare step fetches each original once into its cache; submission verifies the MD5 and SHA-256 from `prepared.json` and stages ordinary sticky project downloads. Volunteers receive the unchanged archive and image files from the project server, then cut crops locally; the run step stays offline ([As BOINC work](../boinc/server/gold-check.md#as-boinc-work-usnm_gold)). A run with Tesseract 5.5.1 and only `eng` installed scored the 60 English crops (CER 0.0932 on BLN600, 0.0276 on Overproof CA) and reported the other 82 as skipped for lack of a model.

### Gold routing

A Gold job names an exact worker image and the base it was built on, so it can only run on an app version built for that platform. The scheduler checks this before dispatch (`boinc/scripts/gold-engine-routing.h`, patched in by `boinc/scripts/patch-server-gold-routing.py`; #747):

- It reads the job's staged `<job-id>_gold_json`, so work queued before the check was deployed is covered too.
- The selected app version's registered Dockerfile must pin the job's exact worker image, and the job's base must match the reviewed image and platform entry in `boinc/project/gold-engine-platforms.json`.
- The native app, `usnm_gold_native`, has no Dockerfile: its `usnm_native_build.json` must carry the physical name with the job's image digest ([native OCR](../boinc/apps/native-ocr.md)), and the base must still match the platform table.
- Missing or malformed metadata, anonymous app versions, unknown bindings and mismatches refuse dispatch with a `[Gold routing]` scheduler log line. Incompatible locality candidates are skipped without stopping the search, and incompatible lost-work resends are skipped without expiring the existing result. Filesystem reads happen only in the unlocked slow check, assignment, locality and resend paths, not in the fast check under the global scheduler semaphore.

Other apps keep their own routing. A refusal can leave a workunit pending for an operator rather than send it to an incompatible host. Adding a Gold worker means verifying its base digest on each architecture and reviewing the registry entries before deployment; that is execution compatibility, not calibration or host trust. Don't change signed payloads, weaken the worker or validator identity checks, or abort or reprioritize jobs to get round this check.
